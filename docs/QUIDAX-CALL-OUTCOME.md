## The gap that matters

This is the thing to be clear-eyed about, because the call was warm and it would be easy to leave
it feeling solved.

> **"We do not require you to be licensed" is not the same as "you operate under our licence."**

| Quidax gave you | QoreID needs |
|---|---|
| A negative — *we impose no licensing obligation on you* | A positive — *Outbuild operates under Quidax Technologies Limited's SEC licence* |
| Quidax's commercial expectation of Clusteer | Affirmative regulatory coverage a third party can rely on |
| Verbal | Written, on letterhead, from the licensed entity |

Two things follow. First, QoreID cannot act on an absence of expectation — they need positive
evidence, which only the letter provides. Second, and more important: **Quidax is not the
regulator.** Their view that you don't need a licence is a commercial position, not a legal
determination. The SEC's view is a separate question and still unanswered.

So the call moved things forward. It did not resolve the licensing question, and your legal
opinion is still required.

## Fair criticism, taken

Quidax Legal made a process point and they were right:

> *"These are things that should have been brought up at the contracting stage, before you
> signed. At this point when you require an addendum, it's a little untidy… It would have been a
> lot more straightforward if you had just reviewed the agreement with your comments."*

Your side acknowledged the agreement was circulated internally without legal oversight. That is
the actual root cause, and it is worth naming once, internally, and fixing: **nothing else gets
signed without counsel reviewing it.** The legal retainer is already budgeted in
`FUNDING-REQUIREMENTS.md` — this is exactly what it is for.

Do not over-apologise to Quidax. Acknowledge once, then move on to substance.

Built almost entirely from the executed agreement's own language, so **it asks Quidax to confirm
nothing they have not already contracted for.** That is the point — make it easy to approve.

> **[Quidax Technologies Limited letterhead]**
>

>

>
> **Re: Outbuild Ltd — Confirmation of Partner Status**
>
> We confirm the following:
>
> 1. Quidax Technologies Limited is registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission of
>    Nigeria as a Digital Assets Exchange, admitted under the Commission's Accelerated Regulatory
>    Incubation Programme on 29 August 2024.
>
> 2. Outbuild Ltd (RC [number]), a company registered in Nigeria, is an **authorised Aggregator**
>    of Quidax pursuant to an API as a Service Agreement dated 13 August 2026 entered into with
>    Quidax Technologies FZCO, an affiliate within the same group.
>
> 3. Digital asset trading, wallet and settlement services accessed by Outbuild Ltd through the
>    Quidax API are executed on the Quidax platform. **Custody of digital assets in connection
>    with those services is held by Quidax and not by Outbuild Ltd.**
>
> 4. Under that agreement Quidax prescribes the minimum KYC standards applicable to Outbuild
>    Ltd's onboarding of customers, and retains rights to audit Outbuild Ltd's performance,
>    inspect its records, and require information on customer and transaction activity.
>
> 5. This confirmation is issued at the request of Outbuild Ltd to support its application for
>    identity verification services, including Bank Verification Number verification.
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> [Name] · For and on behalf of **Quidax Technologies Limited**

**Every numbered point maps to an executed clause:** appointment as Aggregator (2.1), the right to
identify as authorised Aggregator (9.2), wallet and settlement services within the API (1.1.2),
Quidax prescribing minimum KYC thresholds (3.2), audit rights (4.1.6), records inspection (4.1.7),
information requests (4.1.8). Say so when you send it — it is the strongest argument for approval.

**The one point Quidax must actually verify** is paragraph 3: whether the transactions are in fact
executed and settled on the platform operated by the Nigerian licensed entity. If they are, the
letter is straightforwardly factual. If the flow actually runs through non-Nigerian infrastructure,
they cannot sign it — and that answer is itself the information you need, because it would mean
there is no Nigerian umbrella to operate under.

## Why 1 and 2 are the right things to lead with

Legal pushed back hard on reopening the agreement and called an addendum "untidy." Asking for six
things invites a no to all six. Asking for two narrow, easily-granted things invites a yes:

- **8.1(d)** — they have already conceded the substance verbally and said they would have removed
  it. Confirming that in writing costs them nothing, and even an email is useful for your lawyer
  and your investor.
- **14.1.2** — obviously not aimed at a funding round, and cheap to fix or waive.

Get those two, and the letter, and you are in a materially stronger position than you were this
morning. The commercial items — fees, termination, service levels, non-solicit — are worth real
money but are the ones most likely to trigger the "untidy" objection. Better to bank the narrow
wins now and revisit the rest at renewal or at the next commercial conversation, when you have
volume and more leverage.
